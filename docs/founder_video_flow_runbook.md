# IronPal founder video — driving Google Flow by hand

**Status:** Runbook v1.0 · 2026-09-27  
**The script:** [`founder_video_final.md`](founder_video_final.md) · **the config it came from:** [`founder_video_promo_config.json`](founder_video_promo_config.json)  
**What you are making:** eight 8-second clips of one character, 9:16, 720p, spoken lines baked in. The product footage is composited afterwards — none of it goes into Flow.  
**Cost:** 10 credits a clip at 720p, 80 for all eight. Minting the character is free. Refusals are free.

Every prompt below is generated by the pipeline's own builder (`geggen/pipeline/promo/cast.py`), so what you paste by hand is exactly what the automated path would have sent.

---

## 0. Three traps, before you touch anything

All three are measured, each cost a real render on the handlr promos, and none of them announces itself.

**1. AGENT MODE must be OFF.** There is one composer, one prompt box and one Start button. A chip beside them decides where your prompt goes. Pressed, it goes to Flow's assistant, which replies *“would you like me to kick off this 1 video generation, costing 15 credits?”* and then waits for you. Nothing renders. The chip persists across navigation, so check it **immediately before typing**, every time. This single toggle was the cause of the whole “agent-first account” theory — the accounts never differed.

**2. Resolution is not cosmetic — it is the price and the picture.** 720p costs 10 credits and gives 720×1280. 360p costs 6 and gives 360×640. A promo that mixes them has one visibly soft beat, and nothing downstream flags it: the file is a valid mp4 of the right length with a quarter of the pixels. Set the composer to 720p and confirm it on every clip.

**3. An unnamed character cannot be used.** A new character is called *Untitled character* until you rename it. Flow resolves the name in your prompt text against the project's characters server-side, so a character that exists but is unnamed matches nothing, and the generation silently falls back to inventing a person. Rename it before you generate a single clip.

One more, worth knowing rather than doing: **refusals are free**. *“This prompt might violate our policies about generating prominent people”* hits legitimate prompts, on some accounts and not others, and often clears on a retry with no change. It does not charge you. Wait twenty seconds or so and ask again. One handlr clip rendered on its fifth attempt.

## 1. Set up the project

1. Open the Flow project you created. Everything below happens **inside that one project** — characters belong to a project, so a character minted elsewhere is invisible here.
2. Open the composer (the *Generate video* box). If a session panel covers it, close that first; while it is open the settings chip cannot be clicked.
3. Click the **output settings chip** and set, in this order:

| setting | value |
|---|---|
| kind | **video** |
| aspect | **9:16** |
| resolution | **720p** |
| duration | **8s** |
| count | **x1** |

4. Find the **Agent** chip and make sure it is **not** pressed.

## 2. Mint Peter — free, and the most important step

This is where the headband either appears on you or does not. Every clip inherits the answer, and it costs nothing to redo.

Go to **Add media menu → Create character**. The page is titled *“New character — Build and reuse characters for consistent videos”*. It takes references via **Upload** or **Add from project**, and the generate control stays disabled until the prompt box has text.

### 2.1 The identity reference

Upload this file:

```
~/job_stuff/prj/geggen/products/ironpal/reference/peter_01_tanktop_x4.png
```

The first upload on an account raises a **Rights to use this image** dialog. Click **I agree** — it is your own photograph of yourself, and the dialog appears once per account, not per upload.

### 2.2 The portrait variant — locks the face

With that photo as the reference, paste **the whole block** — the background and framing clauses are at the end, and a truncated paste loses them:

```
Head-and-shoulders portrait of this exact person. Keep their face: same bone structure, same hairline, same eyes, same mouth. Do not change who they are, do not smooth, slim or de-age them; keep age-appropriate skin texture. Natural, healthy, realistic skin tone — NOT grey, waxy or pale-green. Replace the background with a plain, evenly lit neutral warm-grey wall — no brick, no texture, no marks, no objects, no other people, and no text or logos anywhere. Framing: head-and-shoulders to mid-chest, squared to camera, eyes open and looking into the lens, soft natural daylight from the front. Sharp and in focus.
```

> The background sentence is not the pipeline's. Its own portrait prompt asks for a *“plain neutral background”*, and on the first real mint that lost to the reference photograph's own speckled concrete wall — the variant came back against brick. Saying what to do with the background that is **there** is what wins, and `likeness_prompt` in the same module already had that phrasing.

### 2.3 The body variant — puts the band on you

Add the **second** reference — the colour crop, which carries skin tone and facial hair:

```
~/job_stuff/prj/geggen/products/ironpal/reference/peter_face_colour_x4.png
```

If the interface lets you attach the **generated portrait from 2.2 as well**, do it. In the mapped API the body variant is given two references — the scene photo *and* the generated portrait — and that second one is how the face survives into the body. Skipping it is the likeliest cause of clips drifting between different-looking people.

Then paste:

```
Full-body shot of this exact person standing in a dark, moody weights gym: matte black rubber floor, a black flat bench, a rack of dumbbells behind, lit low and warm. Keep their face, build, proportions and hair exactly as shown in the reference photograph. Do not change who they are. He is wearing a plain black tank top and the IronPal headband, worn across the forehead and clearly visible: a matte-black fabric band with a thin electric-teal stripe along its lower edge, a small flush lens at the front centre with a tiny teal light beside it, and a small teal ring mark on the right side. The band is plain fabric with NO writing, NO letters and NO words anywhere on it. KEEP THIS EXACT SETTING — a dark, moody weights gym: matte black rubber floor, a black flat bench, a rack of dumbbells behind, lit low and warm — do not move him to a different gym, a different room, or a brighter or emptier version of it, and do not invent a new background. Natural, healthy, realistic skin tone. Sharp and in focus, nobody else in frame.
```

> This wording differs on purpose from the one the automated path sends. That one says the outfit is *“the same garments and the same colours as the reference photograph”* — but no photograph of you has the headband in it, so the sentence fights the thing it is asking for. Here the band is named as an addition, and the no-writing clause is explicit because a logo with letters on a moving band is what renders as scribble.

### 2.4 Rename the character to `Peter`

Not optional, and not cosmetic — see trap 3. Do it before generating any clip.

### 2.5 Look at it before spending anything

Open the variants and check, in this order:

- **Is it recognisably you?** The source photographs are third-generation — a compressed upload, shown on a phone screen, in a 124 kb/s screen recording, 466 px at source. The upscales add no detail. If the likeness is poor this is where it shows, for free.
- **Is the headband on your head**, across the forehead, lens at the front centre?
- **Is the teal ring mark there**, and is it a clean ring rather than a smear?
- **Is there any writing on the band?** There must not be. If letters appear, regenerate the body variant — burned-in text propagates into every clip.
- **Is the gym dark and warm**, with the black floor and the rack?

If the band is wrong, edit the wardrobe sentence and generate the body variant again. Still free. Do not proceed to section 3 until the character looks right — every one of the eight clips resolves against it.

## 3. Render K1 alone — 10 credits, and it is a gate

K1 answers three questions the character page cannot: does the likeness survive into motion, does Flow hold the room, and how fast does the minted voice actually speak. If the line overruns 8 seconds the script needs shortening before the other seven are paid for.

For each clip, the sequence is the same:

1. Confirm the settings chip still reads **video · 9:16 · 720p · 8s · x1**.
2. Confirm the **Agent** chip is off.
3. Click **Add ingredients to the prompt box**, pick the **Peter** character card, click **Add to prompt**.
4. Paste the prompt. The name *Peter* must stay in the text — that is what Flow resolves against the project's cast.
5. **Start generation**, and wait. It is roughly a minute.

The K1 prompt is in section 5 below.

## 4. Check every clip before you accept it

Downloading proves the transfer, not the footage. Three of four clips in one handlr run arrived with a garbled caption of the spoken line burned into the picture and were all recorded as successes, because nothing had opened a frame. Open each one and look for:

| defect | what it looks like | what to do |
|---|---|---|
| **burned-in text** | a garbled fragment of the line painted across the shot or the clothing — *“5ive steps”*, *“nver typed werd”*, *“vetted.”* | re-roll. It is stochastic, not deterministic: the same prompt renders clean on another seed. |
| **the band is gone or wrong** | no band, no ring, or letters on it | re-roll; if it repeats, go back to 2.3 and fix the character |
| **a different room** | a brighter or emptier gym than the last clip | re-roll. The room is held only by the words, since no photo of you in a gym exists |
| **the wrong face** | not you, or not the same you as the previous clip | the character did not attach. Check it is named in the prompt and selected as an ingredient |
| **extra or malformed hands** | six fingers, a hand that changes shape | re-roll. This is why every action below is posture and expression, with no gestures |
| **the line is wrong** | repeated, stammered, cut off, or a second voice | re-roll |

**When one renders clean, note its seed.** A good render is only repeatable through its seed, and a re-roll of a neighbouring clip can otherwise reintroduce a defect you already paid to fix.

## 5. The eight prompts

Paste each one whole. Do not add quotation marks around anything: a quoted fragment inside a prompt is read as text to draw, and that is where the burned-in captions come from.

Every prompt below is rewritten into the masculine by `pipeline/promo/pronouns.py`. The builder writes in the plural because its personas rotate; this cast does not, and a plural pronoun beside a clause insisting he is ALONE is an ambiguity the shot cannot afford. The rewrite works phrase by phrase — a word-level swap corrupts the two sentences that stop the spoken line being painted into the picture, because their pronouns refer to the words rather than the man.

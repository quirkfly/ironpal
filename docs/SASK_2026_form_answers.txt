Section: Product & traction
Describe your product/solution in one or two sentences. What does it do and for whom?

IronPal is a headband with a small camera and motion sensors that watches your set from your
own point of view and logs the exercise, the reps and the weight into your training log
automatically, so you never stop to type between sets. It is for people who lift with free
weights and machines and already track their workouts by hand; reps and exercise recognition
run on-device today, and reading the weight off the bar is the hard problem we are building.

Short variant:
IronPal is a headband that watches your workout from your point of view and automatically logs
the exercise, reps and weight for lifters who are tired of typing every set into an app.

Who are your main competitors and how are you different from them?
What's your unique insight – what do you understand about the problem, customer, or market that
your competitors don't?

Short version (under 1000 characters):

Competitors. Wrist wearables (Garmin, WHOOP) count reps from the arm, often wrongly, and estimate load from body-mass models because they cannot see the bar. Bar sensors (Enode, GymAware) count reps but need the lift picked by hand, drop heavy reps, and take the weight as manual entry. Camera appliances (Tempo) read only their own marked plates in their own classes. Logging apps (Fitbod) sense nothing; every set is typed by thumb.

How we differ. IronPal is the only first-person device: a headband camera plus motion sensors that see the set as the lifter does, so it recognises the exercise, counts every rep and reads the weight off any bar or stack, with no instrumented equipment and nothing to type. Reading the weight is our core bet; no shipping product does it for free weights today.

Insight. Wrist, bar and tripod cannot see the plates; only the lifter's head can. And lifters already type the number by hand, so we need not create the habit, only remove the typing.

Long version:

Competitors. Three groups try to log a workout automatically. Wrist wearables (Garmin, WHOOP)
count reps from the arm, are often wrong, miss leg work, and estimate load from body-mass
models because they cannot see the bar. Bar-mounted sensors (Enode, GymAware) count reps well
but need the lift picked by hand, drop the heaviest reps, and take the weight as manual entry.
Camera appliances (Tempo) read the weight, but only from their own marked plates inside their
own classes. Logging apps (Fitbod and the like) do not sense anything; every set is typed in
by thumb.

How we are different. IronPal is the only first-person device: a headband camera plus motion
sensors that see the set the way the lifter does, so it can recognise the exercise, count every
rep including the slow heavy ones, and read the weight off any bar or stack, with no
instrumented equipment, no proprietary plates and nothing to type. Reps and exercise are
recognised on the device and the model learns from the lifter's own confirmed sets. Reading
the weight from that view is the hard part and our core bet; no shipping product does it for
arbitrary free weights today.

Unique insight. The market has split the three numbers a lifter needs (exercise, reps, weight)
across separate products and solved none of them together, because every competitor is either
on the wrist, on the bar, or on a tripod, and none of those positions can see the plates. The
only place that sees everything the lifter sees is the lifter's own head. The second insight is
about the customer: people who track strength training have already proven they want the
number, since they type it in by hand today, so the product does not have to create the habit,
only remove the typing. Most of the value is in the weight, which is exactly the number the
rest of the market has given up on.

If you were to receive investment of 250,000 EUR, what would you use it for?

Short version (under 500 characters):

Twelve months, three outcomes. Hardware (~40 %): the integrated band with its own camera and sensors, hand-built units then a batch of 200, plus the first hire, a hardware engineer. Validation (~30 %): measure weight reading and rep counting against the bar on the 20 most-lifted exercises across several gyms, and publish the accuracy. Launch (~30 %): Kickstarter, fulfilment to the first 200 backers, the first gym packs. Founder salary stays minimal.

Long version:

Twelve months, three outcomes, roughly in this order of spend:

Hardware (about 40 %): move from a phone strapped to a headband to the integrated band with its own camera and motion sensors. Design and certify the capture module, build hand-made units first (about €130–160 each), then a small batch of 200 for the first backers. Includes the first hire: a hardware and manufacturing engineer, the one skill a solo software founder cannot cover.

Validation (about 30 %): prove the core claim before we sell it. Measure weight reading and rep counting against the bar on the twenty most-lifted exercises, in several gyms with different equipment, and publish the accuracy. Today reps and exercise run on-device; the weight is the bet.

Launch (about 30 %): the Kickstarter campaign, fulfilment to the first 200 backers, and the first gym packs, where the first lifter at a gym maps its stations for everyone who follows.

Founder salary stays minimal; the money buys hardware, data and one engineer.

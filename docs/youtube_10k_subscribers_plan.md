## The idea

People who watch reviews of Strong, Hevy, Fitbod, JEFIT and similar apps are exactly the lifters
who type their sets by hand today. IronPal shows up in their world as **a useful voice in the
comments**, not as an ad. Every comment is about that video. It answers a question, adds a
data point, or shares a real experience with manual logging. It carries **no link, no product
name and no call to action**. The only pointer is the `@getironpal` name and avatar. Curious
people click through, find the channel, and subscribe if the channel gives them a reason to.

## Rules for every comment (non-negotiable)

1. **About the video.** It references something specific the creator said or showed (a feature,
   a timestamp, a claim), written so it couldn't be pasted under any other video.
2. **No promotion.** It doesn't mention IronPal, a headband, "my app" or a link, and doesn't say
   "check my channel". If someone asks in a reply, answer honestly there.
3. **Honest.** It speaks as Peter, a lifter and software developer building a set logger. It never
   pretends to be a neutral customer and never invents usage stats.
4. **Posted by hand.** Peter posts each comment himself from `@getironpal`. YouTube's spam policy
   bans automated or bulk commenting, and a terminated channel ends the whole plan. Claude
   researches, drafts and tracks; Peter reads, edits if needed, and posts.
5. **Pace.** 5–10 comments a day, spread out. No two alike, and at most one comment per video.
6. **Follow up.** Reply when the creator or a viewer answers. A real conversation thread does more
   than ten single comments.

**Main risk, stated plainly.** Comments bring people to the channel, but a channel with one video
converts few of them. 10k subscribers needs a steady flow of new videos on `@getironpal`. Without
that, comment engagement plateaus in the low thousands at best. This is OPEN in the ledger (#6).
